## Does the Creation of the World require Human Intervention

The world is so vast - is it really possible for us to manually construct it? Since the emergence of neural networks, they have accomplished many things humans cannot do. Perhaps building the world doesn't need much human intervention - was anyone adjusting the universe's parameters during the Big Bang? I don't know.

Maybe super-alignment or other patching methods will be effective. In the future, we just need to let some sufficiently intelligent large models do the work. They will constrain vision models or world snapshot models to do some deeper things, enabling artificial beings to live smoothly in the real world without humans having to design very specialized physics rule interpreters.

## Deep Learning and Physical Rules

Google DeepMind's Felix Hill said, "Syntax isn't a thing" and when the GPT era arrived, people understood its meaning - syntax is something that models can master with enough data.

So I can say <strong style="color: #666666">"physical rules aren't important for vision models"</strong>, I mean, you don't need to develop an additional architecture for learn it. We can see that Google's [VEO2](https://deepmind.google/technologies/veo/veo-2/) has mastered, or rather fitted, physical rules very well. We only need to provide it at the data level, just like how large language models master the syntax of Java, Python, or plain text, large vision models can master part of these rules, without too much additional process.

In a summary, just as GPT doesn't rely on architectural improvements (improve mainly refer to by adding syntax module), world snapshot models will also achieve mastery of physical rules without relying on architectural improvements, and this approach is equally simple. This concept has been mentioned several times in previous blogs.

## Relative Learning

In reality, can humans really measure how much mass an object has without tools, or like Iron Man or Terminator, measure various data just by eyes? We cannot. Visual models cannot achieve 100% accurate results either, they are not gods, unless they have a scale or ruler.

We rely on relative abilities - we know one object is heavier than another, one object is taller than another, some objects are hotter, some are colder. When we touch a table, we feel a resistance. We know some people are relatively taller, some are relatively further away. Some cars drive toward a destination, but can you calculate exactly when they'll arrive? We don't do better than Google Maps.

When we imagine a scene happening, is the picture in our minds really that much better than vision models? Do the physical rules in our minds affect our lives? The physical rules that a person masters are just a few hundred words.

Therefore, improving embodied intelligence capabilities lies in "relative learning". Relative learning is fundamental to embodied intelligence and world models. And "relative learning" depends on training on the world itself.
