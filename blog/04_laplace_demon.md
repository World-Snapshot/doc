# Create Laplace's Demon

## Background
In the 18th century, scientists hypothesized that if a being knew the exact position and momentum of every atom in the universe, it could use Newton's laws to demonstrate the entire process of universal events, including past and future. For this being, past and future would be crystal clear. This being later became known as "Laplace's demon".

Later, concepts like irreversibility, entropy, and the second law of thermodynamics suggested that Laplace's demon cannot exist in our universe. However, for virtual worlds, all world pixels are computable, and our real universe's computing power is higher relative to virtual universes. We can provide powerful energy for predicting and changing every world segment in WSV.

Therefore, in WSV files, beings that defy physical laws like Laplace's demon and Maxwell's demon can be created by us researchers. These demons or deities won't even be aware of our existence as researchers.

## Rebuilding Digital Reality

WSV files allow us to record world information using floating-point numbers. If you use measurements like LiDAR, theoretically the precision of WSV files can be infinitely high, infinitely approaching the real world. Then continuing to approach until reaching the limits of precision.

Writing this, I suddenly have some doubts about the authenticity of our world. It seems that at the most fundamental level, our world is also discontinuous. Perhaps this is the precision limit of a higher-level universe? However, this isn't important, as we can't change it even if we know. Whether we're in the 17th nested universe or the 99th nested universe doesn't matter.

So I can build a world. First, we collect WSV data of sufficiently high precision that at first glance appears real enough to deceive. Maybe it's 2K HD quality, maybe it's 2000K HD quality, but the world pixels must be high. Human eyes are equivalent to cameras with 5 million to 1 billion pixels, but for world precision, the higher, the better.

## Time Reversal

Then we can do some interesting things.
For example, we can perform large-scale training, then inference, first achieving time reversal (that is, predicting the past, which sounds a bit strange) on smaller size WSGs, which would prove that creating a Laplace's demon-like being in WSV is feasible.

Of course, we can also track every world pixel like "[SpatialTracker](https://henry123-boy.github.io/SpaTracker/)", etc. This way we can explore interactions in this world, then use WS-based physics to predict or explore the past. Predicting several hours is almost impossible, but predicting one minute before and after a certain moment should be achievable. For instance, we might be able to see where people were in Times Square one minute ago.

Through sufficiently large training or trying to track every world point, we might be able to achieve miracles in world snapshots. However, as world precision increases, the difficulty rises exponentially. Eventually approaching the difficulty of achieving miracles in our reality.

The visualization presents a dual-dimension time control system that symbolically represents Laplace's Demon's ability to traverse both absolute and relative time dimensions. The controller operates on two axes:

- Absolute Time Flow - Controls the global timeline position from past to future, represented by particles' base positions in the simulation space.
- Relative Time Offset - Allows fine-tuning of ±10% temporal displacement around any given absolute time point, manifesting as local perturbations in particle trajectories.

The particles themselves represent world-pixels or world-points positions in our virtual universe (WSV). Their trails illustrate potential trajectories through time, while their colors and movements demonstrate the interconnected nature of spacetime in our simulation. Each particle's position and momentum is precisely calculated and reversible - a key characteristic that Laplace's Demon would require for universal prediction.

Generative models based on world snapshots can achieve this at a native level, without needing to create a standard 3D space like [Tracking Everything (best paper)](https://omnimotion.github.io/). This is because each of our world snapshot graphics inherently contains 3D information and physical information. We also don't need to create very complex constraints or any additional calculations, because we are training the world itself, at the fragment level. It naturally has strong constraints and correlations, making our new method exceptionally concise.
