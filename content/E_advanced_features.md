### Action at a Distance

[Action at a distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_at_a_distance) is the concept in physics that an object's motion can be affected by another object without the two being in physical contact.

This phenomenon (is real, proved by [Bell's Theorem](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-bells-theorem-proved-spooky-action-at-a-distance-is-real-20210720/) in 1964) is difficult to explain because it easily exceeds the speed of light in its mutual influence. In 1905, Einstein's special relativity established a new view of space-time and indicated that the speed of light in vacuum is the limit for all physical interactions. This theory eliminated the possibility of instantaneous action at a distance in physics. Einstein believed that action at a distance was achieved through the influence of space-time.

However, I propose a new perspective: <strong style="color: #666666">" 'action at a distance' occurs at a speed of conceptual level. When I change variables in a wave function, the entire function affects the result. Therefore, I believe action at a distance operates at a speed of conceptual level."</strong>

However, this occurrence isn't that fast. The "Schrödinger's cat" hypothesis suggests that macroscopic quantum superposition in biological systems is possible. But why doesn't it exist? It's because the number of particles is simply too large, causing mutual disturbance and susceptibility to external influences, making it easy for large quantum state organisms to collapse. Quantum states might only exist stably in the microscopic world.
